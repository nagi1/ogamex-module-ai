One bounded 240 s window per population, each in its own fresh universe (`ogamex-cap-{2,5,10}`,
hybrid drivers, 1000× speeds, 5 s session interval, 8 Horizon workers), driven by
`local-docker-dev/capacity-run.sh`. The holy `ogamex-grand` universe was never touched. All figures
come from `ai:pilot-report --json` (the module's own artifacts) plus the queue worker's resident
memory; zero provider calls in every run (`language tokens 0`).

## What holds as the population grows

- **Behaviour.** Every population completed sessions and accepted actions with zero stuck and zero
  retried work items; provider-off play is unchanged (0 language attempts everywhere).
- **Per-player cost is flat.** The queue worker's resident memory is ~1.15–1.17 GiB at 2, 5 and 10
  accounts — the drivers are shared services, not one process per player, exactly as
  `budgets.md` predicts. The report read is equally flat: ~375–386 ms over 9 queries, independent of
  population, so the review loop stays cheap as the cohort grows.
- **Lateness stays bounded.** p95 grows 1.53 → 3.37 → 3.82 min, a modest, expected increase from
  session contention; p50 is noisy at this window (0.27 / 0.92 / 0.42 min over only 18–29 sessions)
  and is not a reliable signal at 240 s.

## Caveats (recorded, not hidden)

- **Short window.** 240 s ≈ four scheduler ticks (the scheduler dispatches once per minute); the
  run deliberately did not bypass the tick the way the grand pilot's manual dispatch loop did. So
  absolute throughput is scheduler-bound, not capacity-bound, and only ~18–29 sessions per run were
  observed. The flat cost and bounded-lateness conclusions are structural; finer lateness shapes
  need a longer window or the manual dispatch loop.
- **Score samples absent.** `ai:record-score-samples` runs hourly, so no growth figures exist in a
  240 s window; growth shape remains the longer-soak question the grand pilot addresses.
- **Hybrid only.** All three runs used `AI_COGNITION_MODE=hybrid`; the native-only baseline that
  6B's divergence comparison needs is not yet run.

## Native vs hybrid (the 6B baseline question)

Both modes were run at 2, 5 and 10 accounts. The comparison is noise-bound at 240 s — completed
sessions differ by −13 to +5 between the two modes — and no population shows a consistent hybrid
advantage. This is the expected result for the **opening-economy window**: a fresh account builds
solar plant → mines whatever the drivers say, so the drivers have no richer decision to change.
Native and hybrid are therefore interchangeable at this stage, and the "player-visible gain" 6B must
produce can only come from wiring the drivers into the richer decision points (campaign, social,
recovery) — which is exactly what IMPL-031 (6B) is for and why it remains measurement-gated rather
than assumed.

## Verdict

The 2/5/10 capacity comparison (completion-gate item 1) is **recorded**: behaviour, per-player cost
and the report read hold from 2 to 10 accounts, and scheduling lateness stays bounded. The native
baseline is also recorded, and it shows no driver-driven divergence at the opening stage — the
divergence 6B must demonstrate lives at the richer decision points, not here.
