# What this source is good for

The **profit definition** a raider uses, the fleet-slot/transporter ratio rule, the reason
fleetcrashing is preferred over raiding, and the community resource-valuation ratio
metal : crystal : deuterium.

## Profit definition (verbatim)

> A **profit** is the amount of resources gained from a raid. […] You can calculate a profit like
> this: total gain - loss = profit.

**ANECDOTAL — verbatim worked example:** "if a fleet of 2 Small Cargo Ships attacks a planet at the
cost of 26 Deuterium, and steals 1000 deut from the planet, the profit is 974 deut." (26 and 1000
are example inputs, not a rule; the 26-deuterium flight cost depends on distance/speed.)
The page adds: "if one of the cargos is lost in the raid, you must substract the value of the lost
ship".

## Maximizing profit — rules stated (verbatim, quotes trimmed)

- **DOCUMENTED** — fleet-slot rule: "You should try to keep an average of (total transporters / # of
  fleet slots) transporters per raid. Below that you don't optimize your fleet slots properly […]
  Above that and you should consider increasing your fleet." Applies "mostly to beginning to
  mid-game"; later "focus more on fleet crashing".
- **DOCUMENTED** — using a whole fleet slot on a single transporter is "highly inprofitab[le]"; a
  profitable target "would thus be a planet with resources enough to justify a raid […] with few
  enough defenses so you won't waste a lot of deuterium on support fighters".
- **DOCUMENTED** — speed matters: "The faster the fleet, the more raids per day you can cram in".
  Small transporters are "significantly faster than the large" but are "basically cannon fodder":
  "The lack of shield and structure points make them unsuited for raiding more than lightly defended
  planets."
- **DOCUMENTED** — use an attack calculator such as SpeedSim, and "make sure to not overkill, so
  you maximize the profit per raid".

## Fleetcrashing — position stated (verbatim, trimmed)

> [[Fleetcrashing]] is by many if not all professional OGamers considered the ultimate way to make
> a profit.

Stated reasons: all resources come in one run (with enough recyclers); recyclers do not enter combat,
so you need not overkill and waste less deuterium than raiding the same defense; most
semi/professional players invest their resources in fleet, so more is in the fleet than is raidable
on planets; inexperienced players under-invest in defense or forget to fleetsave, and a poorly done
fleetsave can still be phalanxed.

- **DOCUMENTED** — cost of the approach: "Recyclers are only half the speed of large transporters.
  So if you want to crash a fleet make sure you can collect twice the amount as you can by a raid.
  Since they are twice as slow as raiding (approximately)." (community statement; **CONTESTED** as
  mechanics — needs official unit speed values.)
- **DOCUMENTED** — "fleetcrashing does not earn you any deuterium. So if low on that you should be
  raiding select targets instead."
- **DOCUMENTED** — if the target is heavily defended, fleetcrashing "may not be profitable, similar
  to raids"; compare gain − expense against the fleet slot or an alternative lightly defended planet.

## Resource valuation (verbatim)

> The standard calculation is: metal: 1, Crystal: 1.5 and Deuterium: 2.5. So 5000 deuterium is
> worth 12,500 metal. This is just a rough guideline though, since all resources count towards your
> score, and supply and demand often even out the differences.

**DOCUMENTED** as the wiki's stated convention — this is a **player valuation heuristic, not a game
mechanic**; treat as **CONTESTED** if used as an authoritative exchange rate.
**INFERENCE** — the page's advice to "prioritize deuterium, then crystal before metal" follows from
that same valuation order.
