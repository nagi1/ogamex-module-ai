# What this source is good for

The **bounce mechanic**: why weak shots are not merely inefficient but *inert* against high-shield
targets. This is the mechanism behind `WIK-013`'s warning that a fodder-heavy wall is "more vulnerable
to [[Death Star]] attack as shots from Rocket Launchers, Light Lasers, Heavy Lasers, and Ion Cannons are
rendered pointless against the Bouncing Effect", and behind its stated need for Gauss Cannons and
Plasma Turrets. Doctrine that says "big guns are necessary" is only defensible if this rule is real.

# Wiki statement (verbatim)

> The **Bouncing Effect** refers to an exception in combat where an individual unit's attack is less
> than 1% of the shield power of a given defender. When this happens, the attack is "bounced off" the
> shielding (hence the name) and nothing happens. This is most often noticed when [[Light Fighter]]s are
> attacking a [[Large Shield Dome]], but in theory can occur with any ship or defense as long as the
> prerequisites of the effect are met.

> the [[Light Fighter]]s will never be able to kill a single [[Large Shield Dome]] without support, even
> if there are 500,000 fighters against one dome, unless the attacker's [[Weapon Technology]] level is
> significantly higher than the defender's [[Shielding Technology]]. To damage a large shield dome, the
> attacker's Weapon Technology must be at least 10 higher than twice the defender's Shielding
> Technology.

# Host implementation — the authority for our ruleset

**Owner direction 28 September 2026: the Rust engine is used exclusively.** `rust/battle_engine_ffi/src/lib.rs`
is therefore the authority, and `app/GameMissions/BattleEngine/PhpBattleEngine.php` is a fallback we do not
plan against. Both were read for this file; they agree on the rule, and the Rust module doc-comment states it
explicitly:

> Shield damage is applied in whole multiples of 1% of the unit's max shield. Shots weaker than 1% of max
> shield therefore deplete nothing (the classic "bounce" rule), but they only bounce while the shield is
> up: once the shield is stripped within a round, every shot … no longer bounce[s] in the same round.
> Rapidfire is rolled after every shot, including bounced shots and shots wasted on units …

In code (`lib.rs`, in the shot-resolution function):

- `let shield_percentile = 0.01 * target_info.shield_points;`
- `let depleted_percentiles = (damage / shield_percentile).floor();`
- the bounce is exactly "zero whole multiples": the shot is absorbed with no effect.
- **A stripped shield changes everything** — once `target.shield` is gone inside a round, the same weak
  shots stop bouncing and land, which is what makes sustained weak fire eventually matter.
- **Rapidfire is rolled after every shot, including bounced ones**, so a wall cannot rely on a bounce to
  stop a follow-up shot.
- The PHP engine additionally refuses the hull-explosion roll on a bounced shot; keep that as a
  corroboration, not the reference.

**Two consequences for the AI module.** (a) A raid planner must not assume it can whittle a wall with
weak shots unless it can strip the shield in the same round. (b) Any module-side combat reasoning must go
through the Rust FFI — a PHP reimplementation is the duplicate authority the module rules forbid.

**Why a fodder wall still holds (the point the wiki makes as prose).** Fodder is not gambling on a
random outcome while the shield is intact: the bounce is deterministic (zero whole percentiles absorbs
the shot outright), and the PHP engine additionally refuses the hull-explosion roll on a bounced shot,
"otherwise massed weak shots could explode a damaged unit straight through an intact shield."

**Reconciliation (the wiki's number checks out):** with the host's rule, LF attack `50`, Large Shield
Dome shield `10 000`, the LF damages the dome only when `50·(1+0.1·WT) ≥ 0.01·10 000·(1+0.1·ST)`, and
that inequality reduces to **`WT ≥ 2·ST + 10`** — the wiki's stated rule exactly. The wiki leaves
"shield power" ambiguous (current vs max); the host fixes it as **original** shield.

# Why the AI module needs it

- **Defence:** a wall built on cheap fodder alone cannot damage high-shield attackers, so composition
  policy cannot be "maximise cheap units". This is the mechanics grounding for `PERS-005`'s target-wall
  question, and it will decide whether a doctrine's ratio is physically able to hurt Battleships,
  Bombers and Destroyers at all.
- **Attack:** it bounds what a low-tech fleet can profitably hit, so raid selection needs it to avoid
  feigning attacks that cannot land a scratch.
- **Doctrine caveat:** this makes the *threshold* a mechanic, not taste — so it may not be encoded as a
  module constant. It must be read from the host battle engine (Gate 1), with the wiki only as the
  human-readable description.
