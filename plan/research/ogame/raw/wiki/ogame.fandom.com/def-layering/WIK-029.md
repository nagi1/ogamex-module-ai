# What this source is good for

The canonical **moonshot procedure**: the 1667-Light-Fighter wreckage, the 20% maximum moonchance,
the recycler choreography, and the stated pushing-rules caveat.

## Basic sequence (verbatim, trimmed)

> To trade moonshots with a partner,
> - Build a fleet capable of winning against partner's 1667 Light Fighter (such as 350 cruisers or
>   116 battleships) and station it at the planet you want your moon located. […]
> - Each of you would send your 1667 LF fleet on an attack mission against the other's superior
>   ships.
> - The attacking 1667 will get destroyed by the defending ships, and the debris will give you the
>   maximum **20%** for a moonchance.

> If not, repeat. Remember: 20% moonchance does NOT mean 5 tries will guarantee a moon!

## Numbers as stated

- **1667 Light Fighters** — the attacking wreckage fleet.
- **350 cruisers**, or **116 battleships** — the stated defending fleet that beats 1667 LF.
- **20%** — the maximum moonchance from that wreckage.
- "**1667 LF produces about 101 recyclers worth of debris.**"
- Method 1 timing: "Send your 1667 LF to arrive at least **30 seconds** before your recyclers".
- Method 2 timing example: recycler travel to the debris field "something like **6 minutes**".
- **INFERENCE (probability):** the remark that 20% "does NOT mean 5 tries will guarantee a moon" is
  the geometric-distribution property (5 × 20% ≠ certainty); the page states the conclusion, not the
  maths.

## Procedure details (verbatim, trimmed)

**Method 1 — attacker recycles:** send a single espionage probe on an attack mission to create a tiny
debris field; send 101 recyclers; LF arrive ≥30 s before the recyclers; "Double-check your times in
Overview. If you messed up, recall and resend."

**Method 2 — defender recycles:** have 101 recyclers on the moon planet; partner's probe creates the
DF; send recyclers so they "arrive at the debris field AFTER the crashing fleet"; verify order in
Overview.

## Pushing rules (verbatim, trimmed)

- "Pushing rules always apply. If you are trading shots and both send and recycle the same amount,
  Game Operators (GO) do not need to get involved."
- If one side wants more attempts and the other does not, "your moonshots may be viewed as pushing if
  the resources flow in the wrong direction".
- The stated safe form: "buy" 1667 LF by sending the resources at the time of the attack and recycle
  the debris, since it is made of resources you paid for. "Just remember that the person with LOWER
  points should always sent the resources before receiving the benefit."
- Alternative: the moon-wanter builds the 1667 LF themselves and a partner crashes them; the
  attacking fleet "must be strong enough to crush 1667 LF, but not so strong as to destroy the
  planet's defenses" (attack simulators suggested).
- No-partner option: "build your 1667 LF and park them at the desired planet, and forget about them.
  It is inevitable that some player will probe your planet and decide to 'rid' you of your forgotten
  fighters, hopefully leaving you with a moon. Of course, whoever destroys your fighters will most
  likely take the debris."

## Limitations

- **CONTESTED as mechanics:** the 20% maximum moonchance, the 1667-LF wreckage size, the "116
  battleships / 350 cruisers" win condition and the 101-recycler figure are community statements.
  Confirming them needs the official Gameforge moonchance/debris rules or the host code.
- The pushing advice is **ANECDOTAL** operator-etiquette ("the game doesn't seem to consider this
  pushing"), not a rule; it is stated by the page as the author's reading of the rules.
- No version or Lifeform marker; the 1667-LF convention reads as classic-era.
